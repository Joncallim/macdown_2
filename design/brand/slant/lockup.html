A leaning wordmark for Slant

The owner chose a 12° lean for the mark and wants the wordmark to lean too. These lockups set MostlyText in Inter Tight, an open-licensed grotesque close to the first board’s lettering. Apple’s SF Pro can’t be used, because its licence doesn’t allow it in a logo. The letters are slanted to exactly 12° so the name and the mark share one angle. Small sizes are shown at 24 px and 14 px text.

Recommendation: bold, 12° oblique, single colour

Matching the mark’s angle exactly is what makes the lean look intentional rather than italicised. At 700 the name stays readable at 14 px, and the mark stays the heaviest element. At 800 the name starts competing with the mark and closes up at small sizes. The two-tone version is appealing but splits the name into “Mostly / Text”. Keep that for moments like the website hero, not the everyday lockup.

  • Construction: the mark’s letters stand about 1.15× the capital height of the name, centred on it. The gap between mark and name is about 0.2× the text size, judged by eye from the mark’s stem.
  • Clear space: at least the height of the T’s crossbar on every side.
  • Minimum size: lockup text 12 px on screen, 5 mm cap height in print. Below that, use the mark alone.
  • Before production: convert to outlines and correct the slanted curves by hand. Slanting makes o, y and x look lopsided. Tighten the “yT” pair, which opens up when slanted.
  • Watch: a leaning mark with a leaning name pushes the lockup towards sports branding, so check it in the BMW M similarity search.
