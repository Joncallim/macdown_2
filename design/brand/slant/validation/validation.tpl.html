Is Slant ready to freeze?

Selected identity: the 12° Slant mark with a leaned Inter Tight Bold wordmark, in one colour (D-010 to D-013). Status: geometry release-candidate frozen (D-018). This page tests the mark at real sizes and in real contexts. It checks the small sizes pixel by pixel, looks for existing logos it could be confused with, tunes the lockup, and ends with a freeze verdict. It explores no new logo concepts.

1 · Read test in context

Every context below uses the artwork drawn for that size: the 16 px microglyph up to 17 px, the 32 px master from 18 to 40 px, and the vector master above that. On a Retina Mac, a 16-point context is drawn with 32 physical pixels, so the 32 px master covers most real Mac use. The microglyph matters for non-Retina screens and 1× browser favicons.

Human read test (to run before freezing)

  1. Show exactly 8 valid participants who haven’t seen the design work each stimulus for 5 seconds, in this order: the mark at 64 px (S1), the app icon at 128 px (S2), a website header with the mark only (S3), then the 16 px line-up. Follow the exact wording in the kit’s README.
  2. Ask “What letters, if any, do you see?”, then “What does it remind you of?”, then “What kind of app is this?”. Don’t prompt with M or T.
  3. Gate 1, logo scale: S1 alone (the first-exposure, monochrome 64 px mark). At least 6 of 8 valid participants, target 7, name both M and T without prompting. The icon and header answers are diagnostic only and can’t rescue a failed S1.
  4. Gate 2, 16 px: at least 6 of 8 pick the microglyph out of a five-glyph line-up. They don’t need to read the letters at this size.
  5. Panel: exactly 8 valid participants. Fewer is incomplete, not a scaled pass. Full kit and scorer: design/validation/read-test/ (D-022, D-023).
  6. Associations: record every unprompted association word for word. If automotive or motorsport associations (BMW, car, racing, sport) come up independently from 3 or more people, treat the lean as needing reconsideration (see §3).

2 · Small-size artwork: 32 px master and 16 px microglyph

Each size below is rendered at its true pixel size and then enlarged. The scaled master loses the V, turns the peak-to-crossbar notch grey, and blurs every vertical edge because of the 12° lean. The small-size artwork is redrawn for its pixel grid. Top edges, the crossbar and the baseline land on whole pixels. Stems are whole pixels wide (2 at 16 px, 4 at 32 px). Diagonals are thinner and the V is opened, so the M keeps its counter.

{{Z16M}}{{Z16O}}{{ZI16M}}{{ZI16O}}{{Z32M}}{{Z32O}}

What changed

  • Horizontals sit on pixel rows, so the crossbar and baseline are crisp rather than half-toned.
  • Diagonals are lighter than stems (1.2 against 2 px at 16, 3.4 against 4 px at 32). This opens the V.
  • In the 16 px microglyph the vertex moves down and the stem moves right by half a pixel. This leaves a clear two-pixel V counter and a clear gap between the V and the stem.
  • Inside the 16 px icon, the V counter is given up. The mark keeps the stem, crossbar and lean.

Tested and rejected

  • Hand-drawn 1-bit pixel grids: crisp, but the 12° lean becomes a 3-step staircase. That breaks the stems into blocks and fights the mark’s main gesture.
  • Master scaled to 16: soft, and the V disappears.

Balance

  • The weight sits top-right (the crossbar and wedge), so the masters sit about 1 px left of centre at 16, and the icon masters sit slightly low.
  • Neither letter overwhelms the other: the M is wider, and the T is darker at the top.
  • Files: slant-16.svg (microglyph), slant-32.svg, slant-icon-16-inner.svg (microglyph), slant-master.svg.

3 · Similarity and collision review

This is a visual-neighbour screen, not a legal assessment. I searched by structure (a leaning heavy M, an MT or TM monogram, parallelogram-built letters, a T badge) and by category (Mac writing apps, Markdown editors, developer tools). This was a web search, not a trademark-register search. A formal image search in the WIPO Global Brand Database and at the USPTO (Classes 9 and 42) is still needed.

The usage rule above keeps the mark clear of it, and the unprompted-association question in the human test is the real check on its motorsport character. If that test turns up automotive associations, compare 8°, 10° and 12° in a follow-up round and keep as much of the lean as passes. The motion is one of the identity’s strengths, so don’t decide the fallback angle in advance. Trademark conclusions wait for the register search.

Lockup specification

  • Angle: 12° for both. The wordmark is Inter Tight Bold Italic with an extra 2.6° slant, not slanted upright letters.
  • Baseline: the bottom of the mark’s letters sits on the name’s baseline.
  • Height: the mark’s letters are 1.15× the name’s capital height.
  • Spacing: 0.2 em between the mark’s lower-right stem and the M of the name. Measure it along the 12° lean, not horizontally.
  • Weight: the mark’s stems are about 1.4× the name’s stems, so the mark leads. Don’t use weight 800 for the name.
  • Letter-spacing: −2.5% overall. Tighten the “yT” pair by eye in the outlined artwork.
  • Clear space: the height of the crossbar on every side. Minimum size: 12 px text on screen; below that, use the mark alone.

5 · Freeze recommendation

Geometry: release-candidate frozen. Public identity: not frozen until four items close.

The mark has passed machine and visual validation: rendering at true pixel size, numerical coverage checks and in-context review. It reads as M and T from 20 px up. The 16 px microglyph keeps the MT look while simplifying the construction, and nothing depends on colour. It has not had perceptual validation (people seeing it cold) or rendering through the real macOS icon pipeline. Optical corrections from either are expected and are part of the release-candidate stage, not a breach of the design record. Four items remain:

  1. Wordmark outlines (production defect): the lockup is still live text. Convert “MostlyText” to outlines using the specification above, correct the synthetic 2.6° on the curves of the o, y and x, and tighten the “yT” pair. Until then, the lockup depends on the Inter Tight web font loading.
  2. macOS icon set (production defect): build the light, dark, clear and tinted versions in Icon Composer on a Mac using the 16, 32 and vector masters, and check them in a real Dock and Finder. This can’t be done from this Linux environment.
  3. Human read test (check): run the protocol in §1, with the thresholds there.
  4. Register search (check): a WIPO and USPTO image and word search, covering BMW M, MT monograms and MarkText. This, not the visual screen, settles any trademark question.

If the read test turns up automotive associations, compare 8°, 10° and 12° rather than switching straight to a fixed fallback. The identity doesn’t reopen.
