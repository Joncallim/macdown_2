Tuning Slant

The owner chose Slant from round 3. This pass tunes three things the review flagged: the lean angle, the cut at the end of the crossbar, and how open the V is at small sizes. All four marks come from one construction and differ only in those settings. Two-tone colour puts the accent on the T only.

Slant 12°

Recommended

12° lean · wedge cut · diagonals 15

MostlyText

Keeps the energy you picked, at a lean the eye reads as confident writing rather than racing. Diagonals of 15 instead of 17 open the V so the M no longer clots at 16 px. The wedge cut on the crossbar is the mark’s one flourish, so it stays.

Slant 8°

Fallback

8° lean · wedge cut · diagonals 15

MostlyText

The calmest and most Mac-like, and the furthest from BMW M. It fills the square best. It gives up part of the energy that made Slant win, and at 16 px it starts to look like an upright MT again.

Slant 15°

Too fast

15° lean · wedge cut · diagonals 15

MostlyText

The loudest. The skew shears the M’s bottom-left corner out of the icon’s balance and leaves an empty top-left. It reads as sport or motoring, and is the closest to BMW M.

Slant 12°, parallel cut

Rejected detail

12° lean · cut parallel to the lean · diagonals 15

MostlyText

Cutting the crossbar parallel to the lean is tidier but inert. The crossbar then just stops, and the mark loses the flick that gives it “pop”. Keep the wedge.

Recommendation

Slant 12°, wedge cut, diagonals 15

Proposed production geometry

Why this one

  • It keeps the lean and the wedge that made Slant win.
  • Thinner diagonals (15 against stems of 17) open the V, so the M survives at 16 px.
  • 8° is the fallback if the BMW similarity check or the read test pushes back on speed.

Still to do before freezing

Should the wordmark lean?

Synthetic oblique, for direction only

MostlyText
MostlyText

Keep the wordmark upright. When both lean, the lockup looks like a sports logo. Upright type next to a leaning mark reads as “text, written quickly”, which suits the product. A real oblique would need a drawn italic, not a skewed roman.
