Four ways to build one mark from M and T

The first board had five T concepts. This round starts again from the structure: each mark has to show both letters in solid black before any colour is applied. Benchmarks are 04 Type T (clarity) and 01 Fluid T (personality). Colour is the existing blue-to-violet, used lightly and only as an application style. Every small size is rasterised at its true pixel size.

Two finalists

What separates them

Crown is a symbol. The M and the T are fused into one symmetric shape: the M lives in the top edge and the T in the stem. It works like a macOS app icon, a shape you recognise before you read it. It is more ownable and less literal.

Ligature is typography. The letters stay letters and share one stroke, the way a type designer joins letters. Nobody needs an explanation, and it suits a writing app. It reads as initials rather than a symbol, it is asymmetric, and it gives up more detail at 16 px.

Neither wins outright on structure. Crown is ahead on small-size robustness and distinctiveness, and Ligature is ahead on instant literal reading.

{{CROWN}}

Crown

M cut into a T

Refine next

  • Balance: the top is heavy. Try a crossbar of 28 instead of 32, or a stem of 20 instead of 18, and move the mark 2% lower in the squircle.
  • Notch: test angles of 80°, 90° (current) and 105°, and depth. A shallower notch is calmer, a deeper one makes the M stronger.
  • Warmth: give the outer shoulders Arch’s soft radius (r 6–10). This brings in the Fluid personality and moves the shape away from the T-shirt reading.
  • Compare leg length 60 (current) with 54 and 66 against the stem at 88.

Could break in production

  • At 16 px the notch is 2 px deep. Softening it or making it shallower would close it, so the favicon needs a hand-drawn pixel version.
  • At some sizes it reads as a crown, a T-shirt or a robot rather than as letters.
  • A notch cut into a T is close to Tesla’s T, so a trademark similarity check is required.
{{LIGATURE}}

Ligature

M and T share a stem

Refine next

  • Centring: the weight sits at the top right, so move the mark left in the squircle and judge by eye, not by the bounding box.
  • Join: decide how the crossbar meets the M’s right diagonal. It can join cleanly, or tuck behind with a gap of about 3 units so the M’s right peak stays visible.
  • Stroke contrast: thinner diagonals than stems, typeface-style, keep the V open at 16 px.
  • Test crossbar overhang to the right at 18, 22 and 26.

Could break in production

  • At 16 px the V fills in and the triangle under the bar disappears, so it becomes “MI” or a blob. It needs a separate simplified favicon.
  • In the Dock it sits among many monogram icons and has less presence than a symbol.
  • MT monograms are common (law firms, fashion), so the trademark space is crowded.

Tests before freezing either mark

  1. Read test: show 6–8 people who haven’t seen the marks each one for 5 seconds, at 64 px and at 16 px. Ask “Which letters do you see?”. Don’t prompt with M or T.
  2. Hand-drawn small sizes: draw 16 and 32 px pixel versions for the favicon, Finder list and menu bar, and check them on Retina and non-Retina screens.
  3. macOS 26 icon: build light, dark, clear and tinted versions in Icon Composer and look at them in a real Dock next to Notes, TextEdit, Xcode and Obsidian.
  4. Similarity search: check trademarks and existing app icons (Tesla, MT monograms, Motorola, square-M transport marks).
  5. Lockup: mark plus MostlyText wordmark at three sizes. Set a minimum size and the clear space around the mark.
  6. Single colour: one ink, embossed or debossed, and Increase Contrast.
